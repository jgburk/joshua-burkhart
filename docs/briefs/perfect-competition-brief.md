# Perfect competition — engagement brief

## The problem

There are three types of crops to plant with different constraints and return ratios. The decision is which crops to plant in the 64 beds, out of 70 maximum across all crops. One or more of the crops must be planted less (6 fewer beds overall). If it's decided poorly, we'll lose money at the market and might not be able to keep our farm. The choice is limited by our budget (20K), labor hours, and a few other variables.

## What I am assuming

For simplicity of modeling, I'll assume all crops of the same type are of equal quality and each fetches the same price at market.

## Hypothesis

Even without knowing the market prices, mesclun appears expensive to grow with low revenue per bed and high fertilizer per bed costs. Further, mesclun has low name recognition which may reduce sales relative to the well known crops "tomato" and "carrot". Therefore, the bed counts I hypothesize will allow us to raise the most revenue given the constraints are Tomato beds=20, Carrot beds=20, Mesclun beds=24.

## How I would know I was wrong

I would know I was wrong if my budget, labor, or other constraints for the project prevented me from planting all 64 beds in my hypothesized optimal arrangement.
